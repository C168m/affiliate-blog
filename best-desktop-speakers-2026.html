Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Desktop Speakers

Choosing desktop speakers in 2026 requires more than just picking the cheapest option. Here are the key factors I consider after testing dozens of models:

1. Speaker Size and Desk Space. Measure your desk before buying. The Edifier R1280DBs is 9.5 inches tall and 6 inches deep—it will dominate a small desk. The Audioengine A2+ Wireless is about half that size, and the Creative Pebble Pro is tiny. If you have limited space, prioritize compact models. I once bought speakers that were too large and had to return them because they blocked my monitor.

2. Connectivity Options. Consider how you will use the speakers. For a desktop, USB or Bluetooth is essential. Optical input (like on the Edifier) is great if you want to connect a TV or game console. The Audioengine's built-in DAC improves audio from your computer's USB port. The Creative's USB-C power is convenient but limits performance. Think about future upgrades—a subwoofer output on the Edifier allows you to add bass later.

3. Sound Quality and Tuning. Listen for clarity in the midrange (vocals and instruments) and avoid speakers that sound muddy or harsh. The Audioengine is neutral and detailed, while the Edifier is warm and bass-forward. The Creative is thin on bass. If you can, test speakers with your favorite music before buying. Tone controls (bass/treble knobs) are useful if you want to adjust the sound—the Edifier has them, the Audioengine does not.

4. Power and Volume. USB-powered speakers like the Creative are limited to about 20W, which is fine for near-field listening but not for filling a room. Powered speakers with wall plugs (Audioengine and Edifier) offer more headroom and cleaner sound at higher volumes. If you host gatherings or want to watch movies, avoid USB-only options.

5. Budget and Value. The Edifier offers the best features per dollar—optical input, Bluetooth, and a subwoofer output for 0. The Audioengine costs more but delivers premium build and sound. The Creative is the cheapest, but you get what you pay for. Set a budget and prioritize the features that matter most to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I use these speakers for gaming?
A: Yes, but with caveats. The Edifier R1280DBs is best for gaming thanks to its optical input (for consoles) and subwoofer output for extra bass. The Audioengine A2+ Wireless works well for PC gaming, but its small size means less impact. The Creative Pebble Pro is fine for casual gaming but lacks bass for immersive titles.

Q: Do I need a separate DAC for these speakers?
A: Not necessarily. The Audioengine A2+ Wireless has a built-in DAC that improves sound from your computer's USB port. The Edifier and Creative use analog inputs, so a separate DAC could help, but it is not required for most users. I recommend trying the speakers first before buying a DAC.

Q: How do I connect Bluetooth speakers to my PC?
A: Most modern PCs have Bluetooth built-in. For the Audioengine and Edifier, pair them via your computer's Bluetooth settings. The Creative Pebble Pro also supports Bluetooth. Note that Bluetooth can introduce slight audio delay, which may be noticeable in games or videos. Wired connections (USB or 3.5mm) are always more reliable.

Q: Can I add a subwoofer to any of these speakers?
A: Only the Edifier R1280DBs has a dedicated subwoofer output. The Audioengine A2+ Wireless and Creative Pebble Pro do not, so you would need to use a separate audio splitter or upgrade to a different system. If bass is important, choose the Edifier or consider a 2.1 system.
