Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Ring Light

Choosing the right ring light depends on your specific use case. Here are the key factors to consider:

  • Light Size and Coverage: Larger ring lights (16-18 inches) produce softer, more diffused light and can cover a wider area, including your shoulders and background. Smaller lights (10-12 inches) create a more focused beam that’s fine for just your face but can create harder shadows. For video calls, a 12- to 14-inch light is usually sufficient. For product photography or full-body shots, go with 18 inches or larger.
  • Color Temperature and Brightness Control: Look for adjustable color temperature (usually 3200K to 5600K) and stepless brightness. Fixed-temperature lights are cheaper but less versatile. Stepless dimming lets you fine-tune the intensity, which is crucial for avoiding washed-out faces. Physical controls are fine, but software control (like Logitech G Hub) is more convenient for desktop users.
  • Mounting and Positioning: Tripod-mounted ring lights offer the most flexibility for height and angle, but they take up floor space. Monitor-mounted lights like the Litra Glow are clutter-free but limited in positioning. Consider your desk layout—if you have limited space, a monitor-mounted light is better. If you need to light multiple setups, a tripod is essential.
  • Power and Connectivity: USB-C powered lights are convenient because they can run from your computer or monitor. Battery-powered lights offer portability but need recharging. Avoid lights with proprietary power adapters if you want easy replacement. Also, check if the light includes a remote shutter or Bluetooth control for solo shooting—this is a nice-to-have for self-portraits.
  • Build Quality and Stability: A wobbly tripod or flimsy clip can ruin your experience. Look for metal construction, sturdy clips, and tripods with rubber feet. Read reviews about stability at full height. For monitor-mounted lights, ensure the clip can handle your monitor’s bezel thickness. A well-built light will last years; a cheap one may break in months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a ring light for video calls, or is a desk lamp enough?

A desk lamp can work if it’s positioned correctly, but most desk lamps create harsh shadows and uneven lighting. A ring light’s circular design provides even, shadow-free light that flatters your face. For professional video calls, a ring light is a significant upgrade. The Logitech Litra Glow is a great option because it’s designed specifically for this purpose and mounts out of the way.

Can I use a ring light for product photography?

Yes, but the size matters. An 18-inch ring light like the Neewer is excellent for small to medium-sized products (e.g., cosmetics, electronics, jewelry). For larger items, you might need a larger softbox. The key is to use the light at a 45-degree angle to the product to create depth. The Neewer’s adjustable color temperature helps match ambient light for accurate colors.

Is a ring light with a tripod better than a monitor-mounted one?

It depends on your space. Tripod-mounted ring lights offer more flexibility for positioning and can be used for full-body shots or as a fill light. Monitor-mounted lights are more convenient for daily use because they don’t take up desk or floor space. If you have a dedicated studio, go with a tripod. If you work from a desk, a monitor-mounted light is usually better.

How do I clean and maintain a ring light?

Dust the LEDs and diffuser with a soft, dry cloth or a microfiber cloth. Avoid using liquids or sprays directly on the light. For the tripod, wipe down the legs with a damp cloth if they get dusty. Store the light in a cool, dry place when not in use. The Neewer’s tripod legs can be tightened with an Allen key if they loosen over time.
