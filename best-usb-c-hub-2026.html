Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best USB C Hub

Here’s what I’ve learned from testing dozens of hubs: don’t just look at the port count. Focus on these five factors:

1. HDMI Refresh Rate Matters More Than Resolution – Many hubs claim “4K support” but only deliver 30Hz. For everyday use like writing or coding, 30Hz is tolerable. But for video editing, gaming, or even just dragging windows smoothly, you need 60Hz. Always check the spec sheet for “4K@60Hz” specifically. The Anker’s 30Hz limit is its biggest flaw.

2. Power Delivery Wattage Isn’t Always What It Seems – A hub might advertise 100W PD, but that’s the total power input, not what reaches your laptop. After accounting for the hub’s own power consumption (usually 5–15W), you may only get 85W to your laptop. For a 16-inch MacBook Pro that needs 96W, this means slow charging under load. The Satechi’s 60W limitation on some laptops is a real issue.

3. Ethernet Is a Lifesaver in Unstable Environments – If you travel or work in areas with flaky Wi-Fi, a Gigabit Ethernet port is non-negotiable. I’ve been in hotels where the Wi-Fi was unusable, and the Anker’s Ethernet port saved my deadline. The UGREEN lacks this, which is its biggest weakness.

4. SD Card Speed Is Critical for Photographers – Most hubs use UHS-I SD readers, which cap out at about 100MB/s. If you shoot RAW photos or 4K video, you’ll want a hub with a UHS-II reader (like some from CalDigit or OWC). None of the hubs here offer that, so if speed is critical, look elsewhere.

5. Build Quality and Heat Dissipation – Plastic hubs like the Anker can get uncomfortably warm. Metal hubs like the Satechi dissipate heat better but can scratch easily. The UGREEN’s tiny size means less surface area for heat, so it runs warm but not hot. Consider where you’ll use it—on a desk vs. a bed—and choose accordingly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a USB-C hub with a Thunderbolt 4 port?
Yes, absolutely. All three hubs here are USB-C and work with Thunderbolt 4 ports (found on modern MacBooks and Windows laptops). You’ll get the same performance, though you won’t gain Thunderbolt speeds since these are USB 3.0 hubs.

Will a USB-C hub charge my laptop while I use it?
Yes, if the hub supports Power Delivery (PD) pass-through. All three hubs here support 100W PD, meaning you plug your laptop’s charger into the hub, and the hub passes power to your laptop while also connecting peripherals. Just ensure your laptop charger provides enough wattage for your machine.

Why does my hub get hot?
Heat is normal in USB-C hubs because they’re converting signals and managing power. The Anker gets hottest because of its plastic shell and extra components like Ethernet. The Satechi runs cooler thanks to its aluminum body, but it still warms up. If your hub gets too hot to touch, unplug it immediately—that’s a sign of a defect.

Can I use a USB-C hub with a tablet or phone?
Yes, if your tablet or phone supports USB-C alt mode (like the iPad Pro or Samsung Galaxy Tab). The UGREEN is the most portable for this use case. However, power delivery may not work if your tablet doesn’t support PD, so check your device’s specs first.
