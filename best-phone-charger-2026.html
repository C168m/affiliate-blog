Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Phone Charger

1. Wattage Matters More Than You Think
The wattage determines how fast your devices charge and whether the charger can power a laptop. For phones, 20W is the sweet spot for fast charging (iPhone 15 gets 50% in 30 minutes). For tablets, aim for 30W. For laptops, you need at least 45W, but 65W is safer for peak performance. A 35W charger like Apple’s is fine for phones and wearables but useless for laptops. Always check your device’s maximum charging speed—don’t overspend on 100W if you only charge an iPhone.

2. Port Configuration and Power Sharing
More ports don’t always mean faster charging. Multi-port chargers share a total wattage budget, so plugging in three devices splits the power. For two devices, 65W is ideal (e.g., 45W laptop + 18W phone). For three, you’ll need 100W+ to avoid slow charging. USB-C is the future, but USB-A is still useful for older cables. Prioritize chargers with dynamic power allocation (like Anker’s PowerIQ) that adjust automatically without disconnecting.

3. GaN vs. Silicon: Size and Heat
Gallium nitride (GaN) chargers are smaller, cooler, and more efficient than traditional silicon-based ones. They’re worth the premium if you travel frequently or have limited outlet space. GaN chargers like the Anker 735 run 20-30% cooler than silicon equivalents, which protects your battery and reduces fire risk. For desk use, silicon is fine, but for portability, GaN is a game-changer.

4. Build Quality and Safety Certifications
Cheap chargers can damage your device’s battery or even cause fires. Look for certifications like UL, ETL, or FCC. Premium brands like Anker and Apple meet strict safety standards. Also consider prong design: foldable prongs are great for travel, while fixed prongs are more durable. Avoid chargers with loose-fitting plugs or flimsy casings—they’re a red flag for internal component quality.

5. Cable Compatibility
Most chargers don’t include cables, so factor that into your budget. For fast charging, use a USB-C to USB-C cable rated for at least 60W (100W is future-proof). Avoid cheap cables that can’t handle high wattage—they’ll throttle speed or overheat. If you have older devices, a charger with USB-A is helpful, but USB-C is becoming universal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I use a 65W charger to charge my phone without damaging it?
Yes, absolutely. The charger only delivers the wattage your phone requests. A 65W charger will fast-charge a compatible phone (like a Samsung Galaxy S23) at its maximum rate, but it won’t force extra power into an iPhone that only draws 20W. The device’s internal circuitry regulates the flow, so it’s safe.

Q: Is GaN technology worth the extra cost?
For most people, yes. GaN chargers are 30-50% smaller and run cooler than traditional silicon chargers, which means less heat stress on your devices and a longer lifespan. If you travel frequently or have limited desk space, the premium is justified. For a stationary desk charger, silicon is still fine and cheaper.

Q: Why won’t my 35W charger charge my MacBook Air?
The MacBook Air requires at least 30W for basic charging, but 35W is barely enough and will charge very slowly—especially if you’re using the device. For optimal performance, Apple recommends a 30W or higher charger, but 45W+ is ideal. The 35W Apple charger is designed for phones and tablets, not laptops.

Q: Do I need to buy official Apple cables for the Apple charger?
No, but you need high-quality USB-C cables that support Power Delivery (PD). Apple’s cables work perfectly, but third-party options from Anker, Belkin, or Cable Matters are just as good and often cheaper. Avoid very cheap cables that lack PD certification—they may not charge at full speed.
