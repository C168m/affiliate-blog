Detailed Reviews

Apple Watch Series 9

The Apple Watch Series 9 is the most polished smartwatch I’ve ever worn, and it’s not close. The S9 chip makes everything feel instant—launching apps, pulling up Siri, or swiping through notifications. The display is 2,000 nits peak brightness, which means I can read it clearly while trail running under direct summer sun, something I couldn’t do with the Series 8. The double tap gesture, where you tap your index finger and thumb together twice, is genuinely useful when I’m holding a coffee cup or walking the dog and can’t touch the screen. It answers calls, snoozes alarms, and stops timers without fumbling. Health tracking is comprehensive: heart rate, ECG, blood oxygen, temperature sensing, and fall detection all work reliably. The biggest pain point is battery life—I charge it every night without fail, and if I forget, it’s dead by mid-afternoon the next day. Also, this watch is a paperweight if you don’t own an iPhone. For anyone deep in Apple’s ecosystem, it’s the obvious choice. For everyone else, it’s a non-starter.

  • Cons: Short battery life, iPhone required

Samsung Galaxy Watch 6

If you’re on Android, the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 is the best smartwatch you can buy right now. The Wear OS 4 software is smooth, and the Google Play Store means you can install apps like Strava, Spotify, or Google Maps directly on the watch. The sapphire crystal display is stunning—rich colors, deep blacks, and it resists scratches far better than the Gorilla Glass on previous models. I’ve banged it against door frames and metal railings without a single mark. The BioActive sensor tracks heart rate, body composition, and sleep stages, and I found the sleep tracking to be more accurate than the Apple Watch for detecting light versus deep sleep. The rotating bezel is gone on this model, replaced by a digital bezel on the touch screen, which works but lacks the tactile satisfaction of the physical version. Battery life is the weak link: I get about 40 hours with always-on display disabled. If you want Google Assistant, that’s your only voice assistant option—Bixby is still there but it’s worse. This watch is best for Android users who want a full smartwatch experience without sacrificing app support.

  • Cons: Battery life, Google Assistant only

Garmin Venu 3

The Garmin Venu 3 is the watch I wear when I care more about my training data than replying to texts. It’s a fitness-first smartwatch, and it excels at that. The battery lasts up to 14 days in smartwatch mode, and I’ve gone a full week with GPS tracking for three runs and still had 40% left. The nap detection is surprisingly accurate—I took a 25-minute power nap on a Sunday afternoon, and the watch logged it perfectly with a recommendation for how long to rest. Advanced metrics like training status, recovery time, and HRV status give you real insight into your body’s readiness, not just step counts. The always-on AMOLED display is bright and crisp. But the smart features are basic: notifications are read-only (you can’t reply to texts), and there’s no cellular option. The price is steep at $449.99, and the app ecosystem is limited compared to Wear OS or watchOS. This is for runners, cyclists, swimmers, and triathletes who want a coach on their wrist. If you need to respond to Slack messages from your watch, look elsewhere.

Head-to-Head Comparison

When you pit these three against each other, the winner depends entirely on your phone. The Apple Watch Series 9 crushes the competition in app polish, notification handling, and seamless integration with iPhone—but only if you own an iPhone. It loses to both the Samsung and Garmin on battery life by a wide margin. The Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 is the best all-rounder for Android users, offering a beautiful display and robust app support, but its battery life is mediocre, and the lack of a physical bezel is a downgrade from previous models. The Garmin Venu 3 wins in battery life and fitness metrics, but its smart features are so basic that it barely qualifies as a smartwatch by modern standards—you can’t even reply to a text. In terms of display quality, all three are excellent, though the Galaxy Watch 6’s sapphire crystal gives it a durability edge. For health tracking, the Apple Watch and Garmin are neck-and-neck, but Garmin’s training insights are more actionable for athletes. If I had to pick one for myself, I’d choose the Apple Watch Series 9 because I’m an iPhone user, but I’d immediately miss the Garmin’s battery life.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Smart Watch

Choosing a smartwatch in 2026 isn’t about picking the most expensive one—it’s about matching features to your lifestyle. Here are the five most important considerations, based on my testing.

Ecosystem Compatibility: This is the biggest constraint. The Apple Watch Series 9 only works with iPhones, and the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 works best with Samsung phones (though it pairs with any Android). The Garmin Venu 3 works with both iPhone and Android, but you lose some features on iOS. Before looking at anything else, check that the watch works with your phone.

Battery Life: Do you want to charge your watch every night, or can you go a week? Apple Watch users accept daily charging as normal. Samsung users can stretch to every other day. Garmin users forget about charging for days. If you travel frequently or hate cable clutter, prioritize battery life.

Fitness vs. Smart Features: Smartwatches exist on a spectrum. The Apple Watch and Galaxy Watch are smart-first—they excel at notifications, apps, and voice assistants. The Garmin is fitness-first—it gives you VO2 max, training load, and recovery advice. Decide which matters more to you.

Display and Durability: All three watches have AMOLED displays that look great indoors and out. But if you’re rough on gear, the Galaxy Watch 6’s sapphire crystal is more scratch-resistant than the Apple Watch’s Ion-X glass. The Garmin uses Gorilla Glass, which is mid-tier.

Price vs. Value: The Apple Watch Series 9 at $399.99 offers the best overall smartwatch experience for iPhone users. The Galaxy Watch 6 at $349.99 is a better value for Android users. The Garmin Venu 3 at $449.99 is the most expensive but delivers the best battery and fitness features. Don’t pay for features you won’t use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the Apple Watch Series 9 with an Android phone?
No. The Apple Watch Series 9 requires an iPhone to set up and use. It will not pair with any Android device. If you have an Android phone, look at the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 or Garmin Venu 3.

Which smartwatch has the best battery life?
The Garmin Venu 3 has the best battery life, lasting up to 14 days in smartwatch mode and about 26 hours with continuous GPS. The Apple Watch Series 9 lasts about 18 hours, and the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 lasts about 40 hours with typical use.

Is the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 compatible with iPhones?
No. The Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 runs Wear OS and only works with Android phones. It does not support iOS. If you have an iPhone, your only option among these three is the Apple Watch Series 9.

Does the Garmin Venu 3 support contactless payments?
Yes, the Garmin Venu 3 supports Garmin Pay, which works at most contactless terminals. However, it supports fewer banks than Apple Pay or Google Pay, so check if your bank is supported before relying on it.

The Bottom Line

After months of wearing all three watches, my recommendation is straightforward. If you own an iPhone, buy the Apple Watch Series 9. It’s the most refined, capable smartwatch on the market, and the double tap gesture and bright display make it a joy to use daily, even if you have to charge it every night. If you’re on Android, the Samsung Galaxy Watch 6 is your best bet for a full smartwatch experience with great apps and a beautiful screen. And if your priority is fitness, battery life, or you train seriously, the Garmin Venu 3 is worth every penny—it will outlast and out-track the others, even if you can’t reply to texts from your wrist. Don’t overthink it: pick the one that matches your phone and your priorities, and you’ll be happy.
