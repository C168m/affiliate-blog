Detailed Reviews

CalDigit TS4 Thunderbolt 4 Dock

The CalDigit TS4 is the undisputed king of port selection. With 18 ports including three Thunderbolt 4 downstream ports, five USB-A, two USB-C, a combo audio jack, SD and microSD slots, and—crucially—a 2.5GbE Ethernet port, it’s the dock you buy when you never want to hear the words “I need another adapter” again. Build quality is excellent: the aluminum chassis feels solid and dissipates heat well, though the external power brick is annoyingly large—roughly the size of a deck of cards. During my week of testing with a MacBook Pro M3 Max, I connected two 4K monitors at 60Hz, a wired mechanical keyboard, a gaming mouse, a 10Gb external SSD, and a webcam, and the TS4 handled everything without a hiccup. The 98W charging delivered consistent power even under heavy load, keeping my laptop topped off. The 2.5GbE Ethernet is a standout feature for anyone who transfers large files over a local network—it’s noticeably faster than the standard 1GbE found on most docks. However, that premium comes at a price: $399.99 is steep, and if you don’t need 18 ports or 2.5GbE, you’re paying for features you won’t use. The TS4 is best for power users with complex setups who demand maximum flexibility and zero compromises. Avoid it if you’re on a tight budget or only need a few ports.

  • Pros: 18 ports, 98W charging, 2.5GbE Ethernet
  • Cons: Expensive, Large power brick

OWC Thunderbolt Go Dock

OWC’s Thunderbolt Go Dock takes a different approach: instead of maximizing port count, it prioritizes portability and convenience. The built-in power supply means no external brick to lose or stash—just a single Thunderbolt cable to your laptop and you’re done. It’s remarkably compact, about the size of a hardcover book, and the 90W charging is enough for most laptops, including 14-inch MacBook Pros and Dell XPS models. In testing, I appreciated how clean my desk looked without a power brick cluttering the surge protector. The Go Dock provides 11 ports: three Thunderbolt 4 downstream, three USB-A, two USB-C, a combo audio jack, SD slot, and 1GbE Ethernet. That’s fewer than the TS4, but for most users it’s sufficient. I ran a dual 4K setup with a MacBook Air M2 and it worked flawlessly, though the lack of 2.5GbE means you’ll be stuck at gigabit speeds for wired networking. The build quality is typical OWC—rugged metal with a matte finish that resists fingerprints. The trade-off for the built-in PSU is that the dock runs slightly warmer than the TS4, but it never got hot enough to worry me. This is the best choice for remote workers, travelers, or anyone who frequently moves their dock between home and office. Avoid it if you need more than 11 ports or require 2.5GbE for professional video editing workflows.

  • Pros: Built-in PSU, Compact, 90W charging
  • Cons: Fewer ports than TS4, No 2.5GbE

Plugable TBT4-UDZ

Plugable’s TBT4-UDZ is the value champion in this lineup, delivering 100W charging—the highest of the three—and solid dual 4K support at a price that undercuts both competitors. The dock has 11 ports: two Thunderbolt 4 downstream, four USB-A, two USB-C, an audio jack, SD slot, and 2.5GbE Ethernet. That’s a surprisingly generous feature set for $249.99. During testing with a Dell XPS 15, the 100W charging kept the laptop running at full performance even during heavy rendering tasks, and the dual 4K output was stable at 60Hz. However, there are two notable trade-offs. First, the fan: it’s audible under load—not loud enough to be annoying in a noisy office, but if you work in a quiet room, you’ll hear a constant low whir. Second, the 2.5GbE Ethernet requires installing a driver (Realtek’s RTL8156B), which is a minor annoyance if you’re used to plug-and-play docks. The build quality is decent but feels slightly plasticky compared to the all-metal CalDigit and OWC units. I also noticed that the dock gets warm near the Ethernet port after extended use. For budget-conscious users who need 100W charging and don’t mind a little fan noise, this dock is an excellent deal. Avoid it if you’re sensitive to fan sound or want a truly silent desk setup.

  • Pros: 100W charging, Dual 4K, Good value
  • Cons: Fan can be audible, Driver needed for ethernet

Head-to-Head Comparison

When comparing these three docks, the winner depends entirely on your priorities. For maximum port count and networking speed, the CalDigit TS4 is the clear victor—its 18 ports and 2.5GbE Ethernet are unmatched, and it’s the only dock here that doesn’t force you to compromise on connectivity. However, you pay a premium for that versatility, and the bulky power brick is a real downside for mobile setups. In the portability and convenience category, the OWC Thunderbolt Go Dock wins handily. Its built-in power supply makes it the easiest to pack and deploy, and it still delivers 90W charging and reliable dual 4K support. The trade-off is fewer ports and slower Ethernet. For best value and highest charging wattage, the Plugable TBT4-UDZ takes the crown. At $249.99, it offers 100W charging and 2.5GbE at a price that’s $100-$150 less than the competition. But the audible fan and driver requirement for Ethernet are compromises you need to accept. If I had to pick one for a permanent desk setup, I’d choose the CalDigit TS4 for its no-compromise feature set. For a travel-friendly dock, the OWC Go Dock is my recommendation. And for budget builds, the Plugable is a steal—if you can tolerate the fan.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Thunderbolt Dock

Here are five critical factors to consider before buying a Thunderbolt 4 dock in 2026:

1. Charging Power Matters More Than You Think. A dock that delivers less than 90W won’t keep a 16-inch MacBook Pro or a high-performance Windows laptop fully charged under load. The Plugable TBT4-UDZ’s 100W is ideal for power-hungry machines, while the CalDigit’s 98W is nearly as good. The OWC’s 90W is fine for most 13- and 14-inch laptops, but if you have a workstation-class machine, prioritize higher wattage.

2. Port Selection Isn’t Just About Count. Look at the types of ports you actually need. If you use multiple USB-A devices daily, the TS4’s five USB-A ports are a lifesaver. If you rely on SD cards for photography, ensure the dock has a UHS-II slot. And if you transfer large files over a local network, 2.5GbE (found on the TS4 and Plugable) is a huge upgrade over standard 1GbE.

3. Build Quality and Thermal Management. All-metal docks like the CalDigit and OWC dissipate heat better than plastic ones. The Plugable’s fan is a sign that it needs active cooling—consider this if you run demanding tasks for hours. A dock that gets too hot can throttle performance or shorten its lifespan.

4. Driver Requirements. Some docks, like the Plugable, require drivers for Ethernet or other features. This is a minor inconvenience on Windows, but on macOS, it can be a dealbreaker if you prefer plug-and-play simplicity. Check manufacturer support pages before buying.

5. Future-Proofing with Thunderbolt 4. All three docks here are Thunderbolt 4 certified, which guarantees 40Gbps bandwidth and support for dual 4K displays. Avoid older Thunderbolt 3 docks unless you find them at a deep discount—they lack the same reliability and power delivery standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a Thunderbolt 4 dock with a Thunderbolt 3 laptop?
Yes, Thunderbolt 4 docks are backward compatible with Thunderbolt 3 laptops. You’ll get the same 40Gbps bandwidth, but features like 100W charging may be limited by your laptop’s power delivery capabilities. Performance is generally identical in real-world use.

Will these docks work with a Windows laptop?
Absolutely. Thunderbolt 4 is an Intel standard, so it works seamlessly with Windows laptops that have a Thunderbolt 4 port. The OWC and Plugable docks also support USB-C laptops via DisplayPort Alt Mode, though you may lose some Thunderbolt-specific features like daisy-chaining.

Do I need to install drivers for the CalDigit TS4?
No. The CalDigit TS4 is fully plug-and-play on both macOS and Windows. All ports, including the 2.5GbE Ethernet, work without additional software. This is a major advantage over the Plugable dock, which requires a Realtek driver for Ethernet.

Which dock is best for a dual 4K monitor setup?
All three docks support dual 4K at 60Hz, but the CalDigit TS4 offers the most stable connection thanks to its robust power delivery and thermal design. The Plugable also works well, but its fan noise might be distracting in a quiet office. The OWC is a solid choice if portability is a priority.
