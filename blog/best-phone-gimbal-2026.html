Best Phone Gimbal 2026 -- Expert Reviews and Buying Guide

If you’re reading this, you’ve probably already noticed that shooting video with a phone alone leaves a lot to be desired. Even the latest image stabilization can’t match the smooth, cinematic glide of a mechanical gimbal, especially when you’re walking, running, or panning during a vlog. A phone gimbal solves the fundamental problem of handheld shakiness, but it also unlocks creative tools like subject tracking, time-lapse, and dynamic motion control that turn your smartphone into a proper filmmaking tool. After testing the three leading contenders—DJI Osmo Mobile 6, Insta360 Flow, and Hohem iSteady M6—for several weeks in real-world conditions, I’ve got clear opinions on which one fits different types of users. This guide will help you cut through the marketing hype and pick the gimbal that actually works for your shooting style.

Detailed Reviews

DJI Osmo Mobile 6

The DJI Osmo Mobile 6 is the most polished smartphone gimbal I’ve used, and it earns the top spot for good reason. Its compact, foldable design slips easily into a jacket pocket, and the built-in extension rod—which pulls out to about 10 inches—is genuinely useful for group selfies or low-angle shots. The build quality feels premium, with a textured grip that doesn’t get slippery even during long shooting sessions. ActiveTrack 5.0 is the standout feature: it locks onto a subject and keeps them centered with impressive speed and accuracy, even if they’re moving erratically. I tested it while walking through a crowded market, and the gimbal held onto my friend’s face without losing focus, which is miles ahead of earlier versions. The quick-launch feature, which automatically opens the DJI Mimo app when you unfold the gimbal, is a nice time-saver, though I found the app itself to be stable and responsive. Battery life is the biggest letdown—I got about 6 hours of mixed use, which is below average for this category. Also, the payload limit means you shouldn’t attach a heavy phone case or a telephoto lens adapter, as the motors will struggle. For most casual vloggers and social media creators, though, the Osmo Mobile 6 delivers the smoothest, most reliable experience out of the box. If you want something that just works without fuss, this is it.

  • Pros: ActiveTrack 5.0 tracking is best-in-class, built-in extension rod is handy, quick-launch feature saves time
  • Cons: Battery life is mediocre at ~6 hours, payload limit restricts heavy phone setups

Insta360 Flow

The Insta360 Flow takes a different approach by bundling a built-in tripod and a retractable selfie stick into the gimbal body, which makes it incredibly versatile for solo creators. The tripod legs are sturdy enough for tabletop shots or time-lapses, and the selfie stick extends to about 9.5 inches, giving you a solid reach for vlogging or group photos. Deep Track 3.0, Insta360’s AI-powered subject tracking, is nearly as good as DJI’s ActiveTrack—it can follow people, pets, and even objects like a ball, though it occasionally loses lock if the subject moves behind an obstacle. The gimbal is heavier than the DJI, weighing in at 369 grams, and you definitely feel that extra heft in your hand after 30 minutes of shooting. The app is where the Flow really shines: it offers creative modes like flashy motion effects, cloning, and AI editing that make it easy to produce viral-style content without any editing skills. However, the gimbal is heavily app-dependent—many advanced features won’t work outside the Insta360 app, which can be frustrating if you prefer using your phone’s native camera app or third-party apps like Filmic Pro. Battery life is decent at around 8 hours, and the charging time is reasonable. The Insta360 Flow is best for social media creators who prioritize creative effects and don’t mind being tethered to the app. It’s less ideal for serious filmmakers or anyone who wants a lightweight, portable gimbal for all-day shooting.

  • Pros: Built-in tripod and selfie stick are genuinely useful, Deep Track 3.0 tracking is reliable, creative app features
  • Cons: Heavier than DJI, many features require the app, some tracking loss in complex scenes

Hohem iSteady M6

The Hohem iSteady M6 is the wild card in this lineup—it packs features that neither DJI nor Insta360 offer, at a lower price point. The standout is the AI vision sensor, a small camera module that clips onto the gimbal and enables hands-free tracking without needing the app. This means you can track a subject while using any camera app, or even when the phone screen is off. The magnetic fill light is another clever addition: it snaps onto the phone clamp and provides adjustable brightness for low-light selfies or close-ups. Build quality, however, feels a step behind the competition. The plastic body has a slight creak when twisted, and the buttons lack the satisfying click of the DJI. The Hohem Joy app is functional but less polished—I encountered occasional crashes and a cluttered interface that made finding settings a chore. Tracking performance is good but not great: the AI sensor works well in good lighting, but it struggles in dim environments or when the subject moves too fast. For budget-conscious creators who want AI tracking and a fill light without breaking the bank, the iSteady M6 is a solid choice. But if you prioritize app stability or premium feel, you’ll be happier spending a bit more on the DJI or Insta360. Battery life is about 7 hours, which is average, and the gimbal supports a decent payload for most phones.

Head-to-Head Comparison

When you pit these three gimbals against each other, the winner depends entirely on your priorities. In tracking accuracy, the DJI Osmo Mobile 6 takes the crown—ActiveTrack 5.0 is simply the most reliable, locking onto subjects faster and holding them tighter than Insta360’s Deep Track 3.0 or Hohem’s AI sensor. For portability, the DJI is also the lightest and most compact, though the Insta360 Flow’s built-in tripod gives it an edge for creators who need a stable base without carrying extra gear. The Hohem iSteady M6 wins on features per dollar: you get AI tracking and a fill light for less money, but you sacrifice app quality and build durability. In battery life, the Insta360 Flow leads with around 8 hours, while the DJI lags behind at 6 hours. The Insta360 also offers the most creative app features, but that comes at the cost of being locked into their ecosystem. If I had to choose one for general use, the DJI Osmo Mobile 6 is the most balanced and reliable. For social media addicts who want flashy effects, go with the Insta360 Flow. And for tight budgets or those who need app-free tracking, the Hohem iSteady M6 is a capable alternative.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Phone Gimbal

Before you click "buy," consider these five factors that will determine whether a gimbal actually improves your video quality or ends up gathering dust.

1. Tracking Technology: Not all tracking is equal. DJI’s ActiveTrack 5.0 uses advanced algorithms that work well through the app, while Hohem’s AI vision sensor tracks without an app but struggles in low light. Insta360’s Deep Track 3.0 sits in the middle. If you shoot in varied lighting or fast-moving scenes, prioritize app-based tracking from DJI or Insta360. If you want to use your phone’s native camera, Hohem’s AI sensor is a unique advantage.

2. Battery Life and Charging: A gimbal that dies mid-shoot is useless. Look for at least 7 hours of real-world battery life. The Insta360 Flow (8 hours) leads here, while the DJI Osmo Mobile 6 (6 hours) falls short. Also check if the gimbal supports USB-C charging—all three do—so you can top up from a power bank during breaks.

3. Payload and Phone Compatibility: Most gimbals can handle phones up to about 300 grams, but heavy cases, battery packs, or lens attachments can push the motors past their limit. The DJI has the lowest payload capacity, so if you use a Pro Max or Ultra phone with a thick case, consider the Insta360 or Hohem, which have slightly higher tolerances.

4. App Ecosystem vs. Standalone Use: The Insta360 Flow is heavily app-dependent—many features like Deep Track and creative modes only work in their app. The DJI Mimo app is more flexible, allowing basic tracking with third-party apps. The Hohem iSteady M6 is the most standalone-friendly thanks to its AI sensor. Think about how much you value using your preferred camera app before deciding.

5. Build Quality and Ergonomics: A gimbal should feel solid in your hand. The DJI Osmo Mobile 6 has a premium, well-balanced design. The Insta360 Flow is heavier but sturdy. The Hohem iSteady M6 feels plasticky and less durable. If you’re shooting for hours, ergonomics matter—test the grip weight and button placement if possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a phone gimbal with a thick case or pop socket?
Most gimbals require you to remove thick cases or pop sockets because they throw off the balance. The phone clamp needs to grip the phone securely, and extra bulk can prevent proper centering. I recommend using a slim case or going case-less during gimbal use.

Do I need to use the manufacturer’s app for tracking?
Not always. The DJI and Insta360 gimbals require their apps for ActiveTrack and Deep Track, respectively. The Hohem iSteady M6 has a clip-on AI sensor that works with any app or even without the phone screen on. If app independence is important, Hohem is the only choice here.

How do I charge the gimbal and how long does it take?
All three gimbals charge via USB-C, typically taking 1.5 to 2 hours for a full charge. The Insta360 Flow and Hohem iSteady M6 support pass-through charging, meaning you can use them while plugged into a power bank. The DJI Osmo Mobile 6 does not, so plan your charging sessions accordingly.

Which gimbal is best for travel?
The DJI Osmo Mobile 6 is the most travel-friendly due to its compact foldable design and lighter weight. The Insta360 Flow is bulkier and heavier, but its built-in tripod saves you from packing a separate tripod. The Hohem iSteady M6 is mid-sized but the plastic build may not survive rough handling in a backpack.

The Bottom Line

After weeks of testing, the DJI Osmo Mobile 6 is my top recommendation for most people. It offers the best tracking, the most polished app experience, and a compact design that’s easy to carry. The battery life is a weak point, but it’s not a dealbreaker if you’re shooting for under an hour at a time. The Insta360 Flow is a close second, especially if you value built-in tripod and selfie stick for solo content creation, but the app dependency and extra weight hold it back. The Hohem iSteady M6 is the best value pick—it gives you AI tracking and a fill light at a lower price, but the app and build quality just aren’t as refined. For the smoothest, most reliable video stabilization in 2026, I’d put my money on the DJI Osmo Mobile 6. If your budget is tight, the Hohem iSteady M6 will still get the job done. Don’t overthink it: pick the one that matches your shooting style, and you’ll see an immediate improvement in your video quality.
