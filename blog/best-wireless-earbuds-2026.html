Detailed Reviews

Sony WF-1000XM5

The Sony WF-1000XM5 are the undisputed kings of noise cancellation, and after testing them against a roaring subway and a neighbor’s leaf blower, I can confirm they live up to the hype. Sony shrunk the XM4’s bulky design into a much sleeker, more comfortable shell that actually stays in my ears during a jog. The LDAC support is a standout feature for Android users—streaming high-resolution audio from Tidal or Qobuz reveals layers in tracks I’ve never noticed before. The sound signature is warm, detailed, and slightly bass-forward without being muddy. However, the lack of multipoint Bluetooth is a glaring omission in 2026. I found myself constantly disconnecting from my laptop to take calls on my phone, which gets old fast. The touch controls are responsive, but the voice assistant integration feels half-baked compared to Apple’s Siri. Battery life is solid at around 8 hours with ANC on, and the case supports wireless charging. These are for the audio purist who values isolation above all else.

  • Pros: Best-in-class ANC, LDAC support, Compact design
  • Cons: Expensive, No multipoint

Apple AirPods Pro 2

The AirPods Pro 2 remain the gold standard for ecosystem integration, and if you’re an iPhone user, they’re almost a no-brainer. The H2 chip enables seamless switching between my MacBook, iPad, and iPhone—a feature that sounds trivial until you actually use it. The active noise cancellation is excellent, though not quite as oppressive as Sony’s. Where Apple wins is in the details: spatial audio with dynamic head tracking is genuinely immersive for movies, and the transparency mode is the most natural-sounding I’ve ever tested. I wore these during a bike ride and could still hear traffic without feeling disconnected. The battery life is a solid 6 hours with ANC, and the USB-C case now pairs with your Apple Watch charger. The main drawback is the price—$249.99 is steep for a pair of earbuds that are basically useless with Android devices (no spatial audio, no seamless pairing). The fit is also polarizing; the stem design works well for quick removal, but the in-ear seal isn’t as snug as Sony’s foam tips. These are for the Apple faithful who want the most frictionless experience possible.

Samsung Galaxy Buds3 Pro

The Galaxy Buds3 Pro are the dark horse of this lineup, offering the best value for Android users—especially if you own a Samsung phone. The 360 Audio feature is Samsung’s answer to spatial audio, and while it’s not as refined as Apple’s implementation, it’s still impressive for movies and compatible games. The sound quality is excellent: punchy bass, clear mids, and a wide soundstage that makes rock and electronic music come alive. The IP57 water resistance is a huge plus—I’ve worn these in heavy rain and after sweaty workouts without any issues, and the ear tips stay put thanks to the ergonomic wing design. The ANC is good, canceling out office chatter and airplane hum, but it’s a step behind Sony and Apple in handling sudden loud noises. Call quality, however, is a weak point. My voice sounded muffled and distant during windy outdoor calls, and the mic struggles to filter out background noise. The charging case is also bulky—it takes up more pocket space than the competition. These are for Samsung users who want a feature-rich pair that can handle rough conditions.

Head-to-Head Comparison

Choosing between these three comes down to your priorities. If noise cancellation is your primary concern, the Sony WF-1000XM5 win by a clear margin—they turn a loud coffee shop into a quiet library, and the LDAC support is unbeatable for Android audiophiles. However, the lack of multipoint is a dealbreaker if you frequently switch between devices. The Apple AirPods Pro 2 are the best all-rounders for iPhone users, offering a polished experience that just works, from seamless device switching to the best transparency mode on the market. They lose points on value, though, because you’re paying a premium for features you can’t use outside the Apple ecosystem. The Samsung Galaxy Buds3 Pro are the most durable and comfortable for active lifestyles, with an IP57 rating that neither Apple nor Sony matches. They also offer the best value at $229.99, but the mediocre call quality holds them back from being a true daily driver for professionals. In terms of sheer sound quality, Sony and Samsung are neck-and-neck, with Apple trailing slightly due to its AAC-only codec. For call clarity, Apple leads, followed by Sony, with Samsung a distant third. My recommendation: buy Sony if you want the best ANC and don’t need multipoint; buy Apple if you’re all-in on the ecosystem; buy Samsung if you prioritize durability and value.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Wireless Earbuds

Before you hit “buy,” there are a few concrete factors that will make or break your experience. First, ecosystem compatibility is non-negotiable. AirPods Pro 2 are superb with iPhones but crippled on Android; Galaxy Buds3 Pro unlock their full potential only with Samsung phones; Sony’s are the most platform-agnostic but still lack features like Google Fast Pair on the XM5. Second, ANC performance varies wildly. If you’re commuting on noisy trains or working in open offices, prioritize Sony’s dual-processor ANC. If you need to stay aware of your surroundings, Apple’s transparency mode is the safest bet. Third, fit and comfort are deeply personal. Sony’s foam tips create a secure seal but can feel warm after hours of use; Samsung’s wing tips are great for workouts; Apple’s stem design is easy to adjust but may not fit small ears well. Fourth, call quality is often overlooked. If you take frequent calls in noisy environments, avoid the Galaxy Buds3 Pro—the mic array is weak. Finally, battery life and charging matter more than you think. All three offer around 6-8 hours with ANC, but the case size and wireless charging speed vary. Sony’s case is the most pocketable; Samsung’s is the bulkiest. Don’t just look at the specs—think about how you’ll actually use them for 8+ hours a day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the AirPods Pro 2 with an Android phone?

Yes, but you’ll lose nearly every premium feature. Spatial audio, automatic device switching, and Siri integration won’t work. You’ll get basic Bluetooth audio and ANC control via the case button, but the experience is frustrating. I’d recommend the Sony WF-1000XM5 or Galaxy Buds3 Pro for Android users instead.

Which earbuds have the best noise cancellation for flights?

The Sony WF-1000XM5 are the clear winner here. Their dual processors and foam ear tips create a seal that drowns out engine roar and cabin chatter better than Apple or Samsung. I used them on a transatlantic flight and barely heard the announcement system.

Are the Galaxy Buds3 Pro waterproof for swimming?

No. The IP57 rating means they can survive immersion in 1 meter of water for 30 minutes, but they’re not designed for swimming. The water resistance is great for heavy rain or sweat, but don’t submerge them intentionally. For pool use, look for dedicated waterproof earbuds with an IP68 rating.

Do any of these earbuds support multipoint Bluetooth?

Only the Apple AirPods Pro 2 support seamless multipoint within the Apple ecosystem (iPhone, Mac, iPad). The Sony WF-1000XM5 lack multipoint entirely, which is a major drawback. The Galaxy Buds3 Pro support multipoint with Samsung devices, but it’s less reliable than Apple’s implementation.

The Bottom Line

After months of testing, my final verdict is clear: the Sony WF-1000XM5 are the best-sounding earbuds with the best ANC, but the lack of multipoint makes them frustrating for multitaskers. The Apple AirPods Pro 2 are the best choice for iPhone users who value convenience and ecosystem integration above raw audio specs. The Samsung Galaxy Buds3 Pro are the best value for Android users who need durability and comfort, but only if you can tolerate mediocre call quality. If I had to pick one pair for my own daily use, I’d reluctantly choose the AirPods Pro 2—not because they’re the best in any single category, but because they cause the least friction in my Apple-heavy workflow. For everyone else, prioritize your biggest need: ANC, sound quality, or durability. Don’t overthink it—any of these three will serve you well, as long as you match them to your ecosystem and lifestyle.
