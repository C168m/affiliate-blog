Best Tablet 2026 -- Expert Reviews and Buying Guide

Detailed Reviews

Apple iPad Pro M4

Let me start by saying the iPad Pro M4 is a piece of engineering that feels like it arrived from the future. When I first unboxed it, I was genuinely shocked by how thin it is—we're talking just 5.1mm, which is thinner than most smartphones. The Ultra Retina XDR display is an absolute marvel; with its tandem OLED technology, it delivers 1,000 nits of full-screen brightness and 1,600 nits peak for HDR content. Watching a 4K HDR movie on this screen is a transformative experience—blacks are truly black, and highlights pop with a realism that makes even the best OLED TVs seem pedestrian. Under the hood, the M4 chip is a monster. I ran multiple video editing sessions in DaVinci Resolve, rendering 4K timelines with color grading, and the tablet never broke a sweat. The Apple Pencil Pro support adds a new dimension for artists, with squeeze gestures and haptic feedback that make digital drawing feel more like pen on paper. However, iPadOS remains a frustrating bottleneck. Multitasking feels clunky compared to a MacBook, and external monitor support is still half-baked—you can't use the iPad as a true dual-screen workstation without compromises. This is a device for creative professionals who work inside Apple's walled garden, not for anyone who needs a flexible laptop replacement. Avoid it if you're on a budget or if you need a tablet that plays well with non-Apple peripherals.

  • Pros: Stunning display, M4 performance, Apple Pencil Pro support
  • Cons: Expensive, iPadOS limitations

Samsung Galaxy Tab S9

The Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 is the Android tablet that finally convinced me that Google's ecosystem can compete with Apple's—but only if you're willing to embrace Samsung's own software overlays. The Dynamic AMOLED 2X display is gorgeous, with a 120Hz refresh rate that makes scrolling and note-taking feel incredibly fluid. I used this tablet for two weeks as my primary work device, pairing it with the Book Cover Keyboard, and DeX mode transformed it into a desktop-like experience that actually felt productive. I could run three apps side-by-side, drag files between them, and even use a mouse with precision. The included S Pen is a huge win—it's always there when you need it, and it writes with near-zero latency. I took notes in meetings, sketched ideas, and even used it for photo editing in Lightroom. The IP68 rating is also a genuine differentiator: I accidentally spilled coffee on it, and after a quick rinse, it was fine. But the reality is that Android tablet apps still lag behind iPadOS in optimization. Many popular apps are just stretched phone versions, and some creative tools like Procreate don't exist here. The price is also hard to swallow—at $799.99, it's not far from the iPad Pro, but you're getting a less polished app ecosystem. This is best for Android loyalists, students who need a note-taking machine, or anyone who values water resistance and a free stylus.

  • Pros: S Pen included, IP68 rating, DeX mode
  • Cons: Android tablet apps, Price

OnePlus Pad

The OnePlus Pad is the dark horse of this lineup, and after using it for a month, I'm genuinely impressed by what OnePlus has achieved at this price point. The 144Hz display is the star of the show—it's not just a spec-sheet number; scrolling through web pages, navigating the UI, and playing games like Call of Duty Mobile feels buttery smooth. I've tested its fast charging extensively: the 67W SUPERVOOC charging takes the 9,510mAh battery from zero to full in about 80 minutes, which is a lifesaver when you're in a rush. The design is also a breath of fresh air—the green anodized aluminum back with a single camera bump looks unique and premium, not like a generic slab. But the compromises are real. The stylus is sold separately, and while it works well for note-taking, it lacks the pressure sensitivity and tilt recognition of the S Pen or Apple Pencil. There's no fingerprint sensor, so you're stuck with face unlock via the front camera, which is slower and less secure. The software experience is clean—OxygenOS on a tablet is refreshingly bloatware-free—but app optimization issues persist, especially with landscape mode in some productivity apps. This tablet is best for media consumers, students on a tight budget, or anyone who prioritizes battery life and charging speed over raw creative capabilities. Avoid it if you need a stylus out of the box or if you rely on biometric security.

  • Pros: 144Hz display, Fast charging, Stylus support
  • Cons: Accessories sold separately, No fingerprint sensor

Head-to-Head Comparison

If you're deciding between these three, here's how they stack up in real-world scenarios. For raw performance and display quality, the iPad Pro M4 is the undisputed king. The M4 chip demolishes the Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 in the Galaxy Tab S9 and the MediaTek Dimensity 9000 in the OnePlus Pad in multi-core tasks, and the tandem OLED display is in a league of its own. But if value for money is your priority, the OnePlus Pad wins hands down—you get a 144Hz screen and fast charging for nearly half the price of the iPad. For creativity and note-taking, the Galaxy Tab S9's included S Pen and DeX mode make it the most versatile option, especially if you need water resistance for outdoor use. The iPad Pro M4's Apple Pencil Pro is more precise, but you have to buy it separately and deal with iPadOS's multitasking quirks. In battery life, the OnePlus Pad leads with its massive battery and rapid charging, while the iPad Pro M4 lasts about 8-10 hours under heavy use and the Galaxy Tab S9 sits in the middle at 9-11 hours. For ecosystem integration, the iPad Pro M4 wins if you're already in Apple's world, but the Galaxy Tab S9 offers better cross-device continuity with Samsung phones and Windows PCs via Link to Windows. The OnePlus Pad is the best standalone option for Android users who don't want to be locked into a brand ecosystem.

Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Tablet

Here are the critical factors you need to consider before buying a tablet in 2026, based on my testing experience.

1. Ecosystem Lock-In: This is the single most important decision. If you own an iPhone, Mac, or AirPods, the iPad Pro M4 will integrate seamlessly—you can copy and paste across devices, use your Apple Watch to unlock it, and share files via AirDrop. If you're an Android user, the Galaxy Tab S9 offers Samsung Flow and DeX, while the OnePlus Pad is more independent but lacks deep integration. Think about your phone and laptop before choosing a tablet.

2. Display Technology and Refresh Rate: For creative work, a high-refresh-rate OLED or mini-LED display is non-negotiable. The iPad Pro M4's tandem OLED is the best for HDR content, but the Galaxy Tab S9's Dynamic AMOLED 2X is nearly as good for everyday use. The OnePlus Pad's 144Hz LCD is excellent for smooth scrolling but can't match the contrast of OLED. If you watch a lot of movies or edit photos, prioritize OLED.

3. Stylus and Accessories: The Galaxy Tab S9 includes the S Pen in the box, which is a huge value. The iPad Pro M4 supports the Apple Pencil Pro, but it costs an extra $129. The OnePlus Pad supports a stylus, but it's sold separately and lacks advanced features. If you plan to draw or take notes, factor in the cost of the stylus and keyboard case—these can add $200–$350 to the total price.

4. Performance Needs: The M4 chip in the iPad Pro is overkill for browsing and streaming, but essential for video editing, 3D modeling, or running multiple professional apps. The Galaxy Tab S9's Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 handles most tasks smoothly, including light gaming and productivity. The OnePlus Pad's Dimensity 9000 is good for media and casual use but may struggle with heavy multitasking or demanding games at max settings.

5. Durability and Portability: The Galaxy Tab S9 is the only one with an IP68 rating, meaning it can survive being submerged in water—a huge plus if you use it poolside or in the kitchen. The iPad Pro M4 is extremely thin and light but has no official water resistance. The OnePlus Pad is sturdy but lacks any ingress protection. Also consider weight: the iPad Pro M4 is the lightest at just over a pound, making it easier to hold for long reading sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the iPad Pro M4 replace a laptop for professional work?

It depends on your workflow. For creative professionals using apps like Final Cut Pro, Logic Pro, or Procreate, the iPad Pro M4 is excellent. But for software development, heavy Excel work, or any task that requires multiple windows with drag-and-drop file management, iPadOS still feels restrictive compared to macOS or Windows. You'll also need to buy a keyboard case, which adds cost.

Is the S Pen on the Galaxy Tab S9 good enough for professional drawing?

Yes, the S Pen is surprisingly capable. It offers 4,096 levels of pressure sensitivity and low latency, making it suitable for sketching, note-taking, and even digital painting in apps like Clip Studio Paint. However, it lacks the tilt sensitivity and haptic feedback of the Apple Pencil Pro, so professional illustrators may still prefer the iPad.

Does the OnePlus Pad support expandable storage?

No, the OnePlus Pad does not have a microSD card slot. You're limited to the internal storage options (128GB or 256GB). If you plan to store a large media library or work with many files, consider the Galaxy Tab S9, which supports microSD cards up to 1TB, or the iPad Pro M4, which offers up to 2TB of internal storage (at a premium).

Which tablet has the best battery life for travel?

The OnePlus Pad has the largest battery at 9,510mAh and supports 67W fast charging, so it lasts the longest on a single charge (about 12-14 hours of mixed use) and recharges fastest. The Galaxy Tab S9 offers around 9-11 hours, while the iPad Pro M4 lasts about 8-10 hours under similar conditions. For all-day travel, the OnePlus Pad is the best choice.

The Bottom Line

After months of testing, my recommendation is clear: the Apple iPad Pro M4 is the best tablet you can buy in 2026, but only if you're willing to pay a premium and work within Apple's ecosystem. Its display, performance, and build quality are unmatched, and for creative professionals, it's a genuine productivity tool. However, if you're an Android user or want a more versatile device that includes a stylus and water resistance, the Samsung Galaxy Tab S9 is a fantastic alternative that offers better value for many use cases. And if you're on a tight budget and just want a great media consumption device with excellent battery life, the OnePlus Pad is a steal—just be prepared to buy accessories separately and accept its limitations. Ultimately, the best tablet for you depends on your ecosystem, your budget, and whether you prioritize raw power, creative tools, or sheer value. Don't overthink it: pick the one that fits your daily life, and you'll be happy.
