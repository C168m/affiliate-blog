Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Portable Charger

Here are five concrete considerations to help you pick the right power bank without getting lost in marketing jargon.

1. Capacity vs. Portability: Don’t just buy the biggest number. A 10,000mAh bank like the Anker Redux or INIU gives you two full phone charges and fits in a pocket. A 20,000mAh bank like the Anker 87W gives you four charges but weighs nearly a pound. Ask yourself: will this live in a backpack or a pocket? If it’s the latter, stick with 10,000mAh.

2. Output Power Matters: For phones, 20W to 25W is plenty—anything higher offers diminishing returns. For laptops, you need at least 60W for a MacBook Air or 87W for a MacBook Pro. The Anker 87W is the only one here that can handle a laptop. Don’t buy a high-wattage bank if you only charge phones; you’re paying for unused capability.

3. Input Speed: A fast-charging power bank is useless if it takes six hours to recharge itself. Look for at least 18W input on a 10,000mAh bank and 30W on a 20,000mAh bank. The Anker Redux’s 10W input is its biggest weakness—plan to charge it overnight.

4. Number of Ports and Simultaneous Charging: If you regularly charge a phone, earbuds, and a friend’s device, get a bank with at least three outputs like the INIU. The Anker Redux only has two ports, which is fine for most people but limiting for group travel.

5. Build Quality and Features: A digital display (like on the Anker 87W) is a game-changer for knowing your remaining power. Built-in cables are convenient but often break. Premium materials like the Anker’s matte finish resist scratches better than glossy plastic. Decide which trade-offs matter to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a 10,000mAh power bank to charge my laptop?

No, unless your laptop is a low-power model like a Chromebook or a 12-inch MacBook. Most ultrabooks need at least 45W to charge, and 10,000mAh banks typically max out at 25W. The Anker 87W is the only one here that can handle a full laptop charge.

How long does it take to recharge the power banks themselves?

The Anker Redux takes about 4 hours due to its 10W input. The INIU takes roughly 3.5 hours with a 18W charger (sold separately). The Anker 87W takes about 4 hours with a 30W charger. Always use a wall charger that matches the bank’s max input speed.

Is it safe to charge multiple devices at once from one power bank?

Yes, as long as the total output doesn’t exceed the bank’s maximum. The INIU can handle three devices at 22.5W total, meaning each device gets less power. Charging one phone at a time is always faster, but simultaneous charging is fine for topping up multiple devices overnight.

What’s the real-world difference between 22.5W and 25W charging?

Almost none for phones. In my tests, a 25W Anker Redux charged an iPhone 15 Pro to 80% in 45 minutes, while the 22.5W INIU took 48 minutes. That’s a 3-minute difference. For laptops, the gap is huge—22.5W won’t charge most laptops at all, while 87W will.
