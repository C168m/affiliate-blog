Buying Guide: How to Choose the Best Standing Desk

1. Frame vs. Complete Desk: A frame-only desk like the Flexispot EC1 saves money if you have a custom desktop or want to repurpose an old tabletop. Complete desks include a top but cost more. Measure your existing top to ensure it fits the frame’s width range (usually 42-48 inches for most frames).

2. Motor Type and Lift Capacity: Single-motor desks (like the EC1) are quieter and cheaper but lift slower and handle less weight—aim for 150+ pounds for dual monitors. Dual-motor desks lift faster and handle 250+ pounds but cost $100-200 more. For a single monitor and laptop, single-motor is fine.

3. Height Range: The EC1’s 28-47.6 inches suits most people 5’2” to 6’2”. Taller users need a range starting at 25 inches or extending to 50 inches. Check your seated elbow height: your elbows should form a 90-degree angle when your hands rest on the desk.

4. Stability: Wobble increases with height. For standing work, test the desk at your standing height (usually 40-44 inches). Crossbars and thicker legs reduce wobble. The EC1’s crossbeam design is decent, but if you do heavy writing or use a drawing tablet, invest in a dual-motor frame with a C-leg design.

5. Assembly and Cable Management: Most frames take 1-2 hours to assemble. Look for pre-drilled holes and clear instructions. The EC1 includes a cable management tray, but you’ll need zip ties to keep cords tidy. If you hate assembly, consider a pre-assembled complete desk.

How loud is the motor during operation? It’s about 45 decibels—quieter than a conversation. During my testing, the motor was less noticeable than my computer fan. It’s one of the quietest single-motor frames I’ve tested under $300.

What size desktop fits the EC1 frame? The frame supports desktops from 42 to 48 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep. I used a 48x30 inch butcher block, and the frame’s mounting brackets aligned perfectly.
